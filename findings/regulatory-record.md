Israel Bonds are offered in the United States through Development Corporation for Israel, a registered broker-dealer. The instrument's distribution channel and limited marketability are relevant to liquidity, execution, and fiduciary review.

## The sales channel

The reviewed materials describe Development Corporation for Israel as the U.S. underwriter and seller of the securities. Aon's ATRS memorandum separately notes limited marketability. These features make disclosure of transfer restrictions and secondary-market limitations important before a public pension acquires the instrument.

## The regulatory history

Arkansas Securities Department records document the firm's Arkansas registration and an April 1986 suspension for failure to designate a principal. The suspension was vacated in July 1986 after the deficiency was cured. The historical suspension is not a current disqualification.

FINRA's public BrokerCheck report contains additional historical regulatory disclosures. These records are part of the due-diligence landscape, but their existence does not itself establish that a current security is unsuitable or that a trustee breached a duty. [Read the current FINRA report](https://files.brokercheck.finra.org/firm/firm_11148.pdf).

## The Arkansas statutory question

Arkansas Code § 24-2-618(e) discourages single-agent or exclusive-agency delegations and requires extraordinary care and caution when trustees enter one. The seller's exclusive distribution role raises a legal and factual question about that provision's application.

The reviewed record does not resolve whether purchase through the sole U.S. distribution channel constitutes an “exclusive agency delegation” by trustees within the statute's meaning. That is a question for legal analysis. The investment record can still address the underlying financial issues directly: marketability, transfer constraints, execution options, fees, and available alternatives.

## What the Integrity Act would add

The Integrity Act would require written liquidity disclosure and comparison with reasonably available fixed-income alternatives. That record would permit trustees and beneficiaries to evaluate the specialized channel as part of the investment decision rather than as an after-the-fact legal inference.
